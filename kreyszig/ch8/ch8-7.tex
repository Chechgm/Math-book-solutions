\section{Fredholm alternative}


\begin{exercise}{1}
Formulate the Fredholm alternative for a system of $n$ linear algebraic equations in $n$ unknowns.
\end{exercise}
\begin{proof}
A system of linear equations is said to satisfy the Fredholm alternative if for the matrix $A$ associated with the system, either of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The nonhomogeneous equations $Ax = y$ and $A^T f = g$ have solutions $x, f \in C^n$ (note we use the suggestive $f$ here, even though $f$ is a vector in $\C^n$, given that the dual of $\C^n$, $(\C^n)'$, is $\C^n$ itself), for any given $y, g \in \C^n$.
    The solutions being unique.
    The corresponding homogeneous equations $Ax = 0$ and $A^T f = 0$ have only the trivial solutions $x = 0$ and $f = 0$ respectively.
    \item The homogeneous equations $Ax = 0$ and $A^T f = 0$ have the same number of linearly independent solutions $x_1, \dots, x_m$ and $f_1, \dots, f_m$.
    The nonhomogeneous equations $Ax = y$ and $A^T f = g$ are not solvable for all $y$ and $g$.
    They have a solution if and only if $y$ and $g$ are such that $\brackets{f_k, y} = 0$ and $\brackets{g, x_k} = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
